01 — The Problem

Telecom is sitting on a
cost crisis hiding in plain sight

Telecom contact centres collectively handle billions of customer interactions annually. The cost infrastructure to support them is enormous, largely manual, and growing. Yet the data that would reveal exactly where money is being lost — the call transcripts — goes almost entirely unread.

01
Cost-to-Serve is Measured, Not Managed $6–$12/call

The industry benchmark for a US telecom contact centre interaction is $6–$12 per call. At 100,000 calls per month, that is $7.2M–$14.4M in annual operating expenditure — before factoring in repeat calls, escalations, or vendor overhead. Most operators track AHT and FCR at the aggregate level but cannot identify which calls are driving cost and why.

02
40%+ of Calls Were Avoidable Wasted Spend

Industry analysis consistently shows that 35–45% of inbound contact centre volume could have been prevented through proactive outreach, resolved via self-serve channels, or handled autonomously by an AI agent. These calls arrive because the organisation did not act on signals it already had — a network anomaly, a bill spike, an upcoming payment — before the customer picked up the phone.

03
Diagnosis Is the Hidden AHT Killer 30% of Handle Time

When call transcripts are segmented by phase, the diagnosis phase — where agents investigate root cause using internal tools and knowledge bases — consistently consumes 28–35% of average handle time. This is rarely tracked separately. When agents struggle with tools or lack contextual knowledge, every additional minute in diagnosis compounds across millions of interactions.

04
Transcript Intelligence Is Untapped Structural Gap

Contact centres generate tens of thousands of transcript records daily. A small fraction are sampled for QA. The rest are stored, unread. These transcripts contain every piece of information needed to understand why customers call, whether issues were resolved, where agents struggle, which offers are relevant, and what a proactive system would have prevented — none of it extracted at scale.

"The data to solve the cost-to-serve problem already exists inside every call transcript. The missing layer is a system that reads them all, at scale, and tells you exactly what to do next."

02 — The Solution

An agentic pipeline that
reads every call and acts on it

The Telecom Call Intelligence Platform is a production-grade agentic AI system. It ingests call transcripts at scale, extracts 70 structured data points from each one using a large language model, and aggregates the results into an executive dashboard that surfaces cost levers with precision and immediacy.

It is not a QA sampling tool. It is not a sentiment dashboard. It is an intelligence layer that sits across your entire call population and answers the questions that determine where your operating cost goes.

Phase Intelligence

Every call is segmented into its constituent phases — Welcome, Discovery, Diagnosis, Resolution, Hold, Upsell, Closing — with time spent in each phase extracted and benchmarked. Handle time is no longer a single number. It is a diagnostic map.

Deflection Signals

Each call is assessed for three deflection pathways: self-serve channel eligibility, agentic AI resolvability, and proactive outreach applicability. The system identifies not just whether a call was deflectable, but how it could have been prevented.

Agent Performance

Agent skill rating, tool struggle evidence, troubleshooting path accuracy, and empathy statement count are extracted per call. Coaching opportunities surface automatically, tied directly to the transcripts that evidence them.

03 — Agent Architecture

Seven-node LangGraph pipeline
with agentic control loops

The system is implemented as a LangGraph state graph — an agentic orchestration pattern where each node is a discrete, auditable step. State accumulates through the graph and every intermediate output is traceable. Two Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite calls per run: one for ReAct-guided extraction and one for the InsightsAgent deliberation loop. The pipeline is CI-verified across 198 unit tests and secured with a five-component security layer at every agent boundary.

Why LangGraph?

LangGraph provides a stateful, auditable graph execution model. Each node receives the accumulated state and returns an updated version — no hidden side effects. The 7-node graph includes conditional routing (QualityGate bypasses aggregation on catastrophic failure), a human approval gate before export, and LangSmith tracing. State is fully inspectable at every step, which matters for enterprise audit trails.

Why Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite?

Google AI Studio's free tier provides 15 requests/minute — sufficient for one 20-call batch per day on the free tier; upgrade to gemini-2.0-flash-lite (1,500 RPD) for multi-batch production runs. thinking_budget=0 prevents thinking tokens from consuming the extraction budget. Chain-of-Thought reasoning is injected into the JSON schema itself, producing step-by-step reasoning before every field extraction with no additional API calls.

Cost to Serve

Covers Phases 1–4. This is where the majority of AHT lives. Phase-level timing reveals whether handle time is driven by authentication friction, over-long discovery, agent tool struggle in diagnosis, or resolution complexity. Each phase has a different optimisation lever: IVR improvements (Ph1), structured probing (Ph2), knowledge base quality (Ph3), script effectiveness (Ph4).

Cost to Sell

Covers Phase 5. Every upsell attempt is evaluated: was it relevant to the customer's eligibility? Was it scripted or personalised? What was the outcome? These signals, aggregated across thousands of calls, quantify revenue leakage from untargeted upsell and identify which issue categories have the highest cross-sell adjacency.

Cost to Retain

Covers Phase 6 and relationship building signals throughout. Sentiment trajectory from call start to close, repeat call risk, dissatisfaction signals, and FCR together determine whether each interaction built or eroded customer loyalty. High repeat-call risk at closing is the leading indicator of churn, not CSAT surveys.

08 — Why Now

Three forces converging
right now

⚡

Foundation Models Are Enterprise-Ready

Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite, Claude, and GPT-4o have crossed the threshold where structured JSON extraction from long call transcripts is reliable, auditable, and cost-effective at scale. Twelve months ago, this required bespoke ML pipelines. Today it runs on Google AI Studio's free tier for prototyping and pennies per thousand calls in production.

🤖

Agentic AI Is Production-Ready

LangGraph, ReAct loops, and deliberation patterns have matured to the point where a 7-node pipeline with retry logic, state management, security, and auditability can be built and CI-verified in days, not months. Telecom operators that build this intelligence layer now have a 2–3 year advantage over those waiting for a vendor to package it.

📉

Telecom OpEx Pressure Is Structural

ARPU compression, increased competition from MVNOs, and rising vendor costs have made contact centre cost reduction a board-level priority across every major telecom operator. The window for building proprietary AI capability — before commodity solutions lock in vendor dependence — is now.

"The organisations that win the next decade of telecom will not be those with the most calls. They will be those with the deepest understanding of what every call actually contains — and the systems to act on that understanding at scale."

The industry context

Customer care is telecom's largest
controllable operating cost.

~$1.7T
Global telecom services market — and ARPU is flat or declining in most regions. Margin must come from cost.
Billions
of care contacts per year. A tier-1 operator with 50–100M subscribers handles 100M+ assisted contacts annually.
$6–12

Care experience also drives churn and NPS: a single unresolved call measurably raises a subscriber's propensity to leave. Cost-to-serve is therefore both a margin lever and a retention lever — every avoidable, repeated, or escalated call leaks both.

Figures are widely used industry benchmark ranges; the engine replaces them with an operator's actual ACD cost data in production.
The problem

Operators manage cost-to-serve in aggregate —
but the drivers live inside the conversation.

  • QA listens to 1–2% of calls. Manual sampling means 98% of conversations are never analyzed for cost drivers.
  • ACD data says how long, not why. Handle time, queue, and disposition codes can't tell you a call was avoidable.
  • Deflection programs run on intuition. Which intents to automate is decided by anecdote, not evidence.
20–40%
of inbound telecom contacts are commonly estimated to be avoidable — billing confusion, status checks, information-only queries. At $6–12 per call, that is structural margin leakage hiding in plain sight.
The question is no longer "what is our cost per call?" but "which calls should never have happened, and what resolves the rest without an agent?"
The solution

Read every call. Quantify every driver.

Agentic AI · Layer 1

Agents that analyze:
why this isn't "a script calling an API."

ReAct control loop

The extraction agent scores its own field coverage after every call. Below threshold, it reasons about what's missing and issues a targeted re-query — recovering critical fields instead of shipping nulls into the KPIs.

Self-critique deliberation

The insights agent drafts recommendations, then a critique pass grades each one for data-grounding and specificity, and a synthesis pass rewrites the weak ones. Generic consulting filler doesn't survive.

Autonomous degradation

Provider down? Quota exhausted? Agents fall back across a NIM → Claude → rule-based chain, circuit-break across batch processes, and checkpoint so no work is repeated.

Every one of these behaviours emits a decision record — what was decided, why, the evidence, and the alternatives rejected. The reasoning is auditable end to end, which is what separates deployable agentic systems from demos.

Why per-call classification matters

Aggregate deflection targets fail because they automate the wrong intents. Classifying every call against each lever means the business case is built from evidence — intent by intent, dollar by dollar — before a single journey is rebuilt.

Agentic AI · Layer 2

Agents that resolve:
the intervention the analysis de-risks.

Tier-1 operators already run conversational assistants (Vodafone's TOBi, Deutsche Telekom's Frag Magenta, and peers). The industry's next step is agentic: assistants that don't just answer but act — checking a line, re-provisioning a service, adjusting a bill, scheduling a technician.

Industry analysts project that agentic AI will autonomously resolve the majority of common service issues by the late 2020s. The operators that win that transition are the ones that know — with evidence — which intents to hand to agents first.

  • This engine is the targeting system. It identifies the AI-resolvable ~40% and ranks intents by volume × cost × risk.
  • It is also the measurement system. Re-run continuously, it shows deflected volume, FCR shift, and sentiment impact after each rollout wave.
  • And the safety case. Escalation, sentiment, and repeat-risk fields show where AI should not be deployed yet.
Analyze → target → deploy → re-measure: the closed loop that turns agentic AI from a pilot into a P&L line.

Enterprise readiness

Governed by design — because telecom
regulators will ask, and boards should.

PII redaction at the gate

Emails, card numbers, and identifiers are scanned and redacted before any transcript reaches an LLM. Redactions are logged per call.

Hard cost controls

A budget guard halts any run that exceeds its spend ceiling; rate limiters and quota circuit-breakers contain provider failures.

Quality gates

Every extraction is QA-scored on a 100-point model; low-confidence records are excluded from KPIs rather than silently averaged in.

Full decision audit

Every agent decision — what, why, evidence, alternatives — is exported as a machine-readable trail for audit and model-risk review.

Human approval gate

An optional human sign-off node sits before export — autonomy where it's safe, oversight where it matters.

Engineering discipline

342 automated tests, typed codebase, CI on every change. The governance is tested, not aspirational.

Designed to align with emerging AI-governance expectations (EU AI Act-style transparency, sector regulators) without slowing the analysis loop.

SECTION 1WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

1 · PURPOSE AND SCOPE

A complete account: problem,
system, evidence, and limits.

Written for operations and technology leadership at telecom operators — and for anyone evaluating how a governed AI system should be designed, explained, and validated.

The document moves in order: why care conversations matter economically, why they go unread, what the system does about it, and what happened when it ran. Every figure is sourced from a June 2026 production run unless noted, and every assumption is labelled where it is used.

Limitations appear before the results they qualify, not after.

IN SCOPE

Reads recorded transcripts · extracts ~70 structured fields per call · scores its own extraction quality · aggregates indicators · prices reduction levers · writes strategy recommendations and a complete audit trail.

DELIBERATELY OUT OF SCOPE

It never speaks to customers, never modifies accounts, never touches operator systems. It is an analysis system — a boundary chosen so that autonomy can be earned in stages. Section 5 returns to this.

SECTION 3THE PROBLEM · VISIBILITY

3 · THE MEASUREMENT GAP

The tools measure how long.
Only the conversation knows why.

Operators are well instrumented around the call, not inside it. Each source answers its own question — none answers the one that reduces cost: why did this call exist, and what would have prevented it?

WHAT "AGENTIC" MEANS IN THIS DOCUMENT

Software components — agents — that are given a goal, choose among permitted actions, observe the result, and adjust, rather than executing a fixed script. Concretely here: an agent re-queries when its own output is incomplete, critiques and rewrites its own analysis, and falls back across providers on failure. Every such choice is logged.

Three shifts arrived together: small fast models made full coverage economical, structured-output techniques made 70-field extraction dependable, and orchestration frameworks made multi-agent workflows ordinary engineering.

SECTION 5THE SYSTEM · CONCEPT

5 · THE IDEA, IN ONE PAGE

Turn every conversation into
structured economics.

Each transcript becomes ~70 fields — what the call was about, how each phase spent its seconds, whether it was resolved, whether it was preventable. Aggregated, those fields become indicators and priced reduction levers. Four principles shaped everything that follows:

P1Analyse, don't act. The system reads; it never touches customers or operator systems. Failure costs a re-run, not an incident — so autonomy can be earned in stages.
P2Read everything, trust selectively. Full coverage is the point, but every extraction is quality-scored, and failing records are excluded — never averaged in.
P3Every machine decision on the record. What was decided, why, on what evidence, and what was rejected — auditable the way a financial process is auditable.
P4Match model size to task. A small fast model reads at volume; a large model reasons once over the aggregate. Cost scales with calls; reasoning doesn't have to.
Sections 6–9 implement these principles · Sections 10–13 test them against a production run07 / 18
SECTION 6THE SYSTEM · ARCHITECTURE

6 · HOW IT WORKS

Seven stations. One governed pass.
Nothing unverified reaches a decision-maker.

A seven-node LangGraph state machine: each node is a stateless agent that performs one responsibility, appends its outputs and decision records, and hands state forward immutably. After quality scoring, a conditional route diverts failing datasets to a protective export path.

Why a graph, not a script: conditional routing, retries, and checkpoint-resume need explicit state and edges. A failed batch resumes from its checkpoint instead of re-spending budget — which mattered in practice during validation.

Why two models: Claude Haiku 4.5 reads at ~$0.01/call and 50 calls/minute; Llama 3.3 70B (NVIDIA NIM) reasons once per batch. Provider choice is one configuration constant — pricing, rate limits, and key checks follow from it.

Stack: LangGraph · Claude Haiku 4.5 · Llama 3.3 70B · Streamlit · FastAPI · 342-test CI suite08 / 18
SECTION 7THE SYSTEM · AGENTS 1–3

7 · THE AGENTS — PREPARE, READ, VERIFY

One responsibility each, explicit
thresholds, and a reason to exist.

TABLE 7.1 — WHAT EACH AGENT DOES, AND WHY IT WAS INCLUDED
AGENTWHAT IT DOESWHY IT EXISTS
1 · Ingestion Loads transcripts; rejects those under 150 characters or 4 turns; scans and redacts PII before any text reaches a model; logs every rejection and redaction. Bad input corrupts every downstream number — so gating comes first. PII redaction lives here so no external model ever sees unmasked customer data.
2 · Extraction Submits each transcript against the ~70-field schema; scores its own output on 7 critical fields; below 70% coverage, re-queries just the missing fields (up to twice), then merges. Models sometimes return incomplete JSON. Unrepaired, those nulls silently bias the indicators; targeted re-query repairs them at an eighth of a full re-read's cost.
3 · Quality Audits each extraction on 100 points — completeness 30, enum validity 25, cross-field consistency 25, plausibility 20. Below 60, the record is excluded; a dataset-level gate routes wholesale failures away from publication. An analysis system must know when its own output is untrustworthy. Consistency rules ("resolved first call" cannot coexist with "escalated") catch errors no single-field check would find.
Every threshold is a named configuration constant; every decision these agents take is written to the decision log09 / 18
SECTION 8THE SYSTEM · AGENTS 4–6

8 · THE AGENTS — PRICE, REASON, PUBLISH

From verified fields to indicators,
strategy, and an audit trail.

TABLE 8.1 — AGENTS 4–6 AND THE APPROVAL GATE
AGENTWHAT IT DOESWHY IT EXISTS
4 · Aggregation Computes indicators (resolution, avoidability, resolvability, phase timings), distributions, and the three-lever economic model — from quality-passed records only. Numbers built on unverified records inherit their errors. Aggregating only graded records is what makes the output defensible to a finance audience.
5 · Insights A 70B model analyses the aggregate; a second pass critiques each recommendation for grounding and specificity; a third rewrites the weak ones. Falls back NIM → Claude → rules. Single-pass strategy output drifts toward generic consulting language; the critique pass removes it. The fallback chain means the pipeline finishes even with two providers down.
Approval gate Optional human sign-off between insights and export, with a timeout policy. Some organisations require a person in the loop. Here that is a configuration switch, not a process retrofitted later.
6 · Export Writes dashboard data, per-call CSV, QA report, decision log, and an immutable run manifest. On the failure path it preserves the last verified dashboard rather than overwriting it. The manifest makes every run reproducible and disputable. The protective path exists because a corrupted executive dashboard destroys more trust than a late one.
Insights record their provider, pass count, and critique verdict inside the output itself; per-pass model usage is metered10 / 18

SECTION 15CONCLUSIONS

15 · CONCLUSIONS

What the work demonstrates.

C1Full-coverage conversation analysis is economically viable today. Measured at $0.013 per call — roughly 0.2% of handling cost, below the price of the 2% human sample it replaces.
C2Structured extraction is dependable when it is quality-gated. Zero failures across the run; every record graded; only graded records reach the indicators.
C3Phase timing reveals structure that duration metrics cannot. Roughly 40% of call time precedes problem-solving — invisible to ACD data, addressable by known interventions.
C4Preventable and automatable contacts can be identified call by call. Classifications arrive as lists of named calls with intent and product context — implementable, not just reportable.
C5Agentic autonomy can be governed to audit standard. Budget ceilings, quality gates, PII boundaries, and a complete decision log held under production conditions.
Each conclusion traces to the sections above and to the versioned artifacts of the June 2026 run17 / 18
SECTION 16THE NEXT STEP

16 · RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP

Four weeks, your transcripts,
your numbers.

The corpus results establish that the method works; they deliberately do not claim what any operator's numbers will be. The smallest decision that resolves the question is phase 0: thirty to ninety days of historical transcripts, analysed under the governance of Section 9, returning the operator's own call anatomy, indicators, and sized levers.

It requires a transcript export and a data-protection review — no integration, no customer-facing change, no commitment beyond the four weeks.

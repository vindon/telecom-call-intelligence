SECTION 1PURPOSE AND SCOPE
1 · PURPOSE AND SCOPE

What this document is, who it is for, and how to read it.

Subject. Customer care conversations contain detailed evidence about why customers call, how time is spent, and which contacts could be prevented or automated. That evidence is rarely analysed at scale. This document describes a working system that performs this analysis, and the results of running it.

Audience. Operations and technology leadership at telecom operators, and any reader evaluating whether conversation-level analytics justifies a pilot — or evaluating the author's approach to building governed AI systems.

Method of the document. Context first, then the system and the reasoning behind each component, then measured evidence, then interpretation. Claims are tied to sources; planning assumptions are labelled as such.

SCOPE — WHAT THE SYSTEM DOES

Reads recorded call transcripts; extracts ~70 structured fields per call; scores its own extraction quality; aggregates operational indicators; estimates economic levers; produces strategy recommendations and a full audit trail.

OUT OF SCOPE — WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

It does not interact with customers, modify accounts, or take any action on operator systems. It is an analysis system. This boundary is a deliberate design choice discussed in Section 5.

SECTION 2BACKGROUND
2 · BACKGROUND

The economics of assisted customer care.

Customer care is among the largest controllable cost lines a telecom operator manages, and its unit economics are unusually transparent: every contact has a channel, and every channel has a cost.

CONTACT CHANNELTYPICAL UNIT COSTNOTES
Assisted voice call$6 – 12Agent time, telephony, occupancy, supervision
Assisted chat$2 – 5Concurrency lowers cost per contact
Self-service (app/IVR/web)< $0.50Near-zero marginal cost once built

Commonly cited industry ranges; an operator's ACD and workforce data replaces these in any serious analysis.

Volumes make the stakes concrete. A tier-1 operator with 50–100M subscribers handles on the order of 100M assisted contacts a year; at the unit costs opposite, annual care expenditure reaches $0.6–1.2B. A national operator carries proportionally smaller but structurally identical exposure.

Two further properties matter for what follows. First, care interactions correlate with churn: unresolved or repeated contacts measurably raise leave-propensity, so cost and retention move together. Second, industry studies consistently estimate that 20–40% of inbound contacts are avoidable — driven by billing confusion, status checks, and information-only queries that better journeys would prevent.

Section 3 examines why this avoidable share is difficult to locate with existing tooling04 / 18
SECTION 3THE MEASUREMENT GAP
3 · THE MEASUREMENT GAP

Existing instrumentation measures duration and sentiment — not cause.

Operators are well instrumented around the call but not inside it. Each existing source answers a different question, and none answers the one that drives cost reduction: why did this call exist, and what would have prevented it?

TABLE 3.1 — WHAT EACH EXISTING SOURCE CAN AND CANNOT ANSWER
SOURCEANSWERSCANNOT ANSWER
ACD / telephony dataHow long, which queue, what disposition codeWhether the call was preventable; where inside the call time was lost
Post-call surveysHow the customer felt afterwardsWhat occurred during the call; responses skew to extremes
Human QA reviewAgent behaviour and compliance on the reviewed sampleAnything about the other 98–99% — sampling exists to coach agents, not to find structural cost
Speech analytics (keyword)Mentions of configured phrasesMulti-step reasoning: avoidability, resolvability, cross-field consistency

The consequence is practical: deflection and automation roadmaps are typically prioritised in workshops, from anecdote and intuition, because the primary evidence — the conversations — is unread at scale.

The information needed exists in every recorded call; the constraint has been the cost of reading them05 / 18

DEFINITION — "AGENTIC" AS USED IN THIS DOCUMENT

An agentic system is one in which software components (agents) are given a goal, decide among permitted actions, observe the result, and adjust — rather than executing a fixed script. In this system that means concretely: an agent re-queries when its own output is incomplete, critiques and rewrites its own analysis, and falls back across providers on failure. Each such decision is recorded.

Three enabling shifts arrived together: small fast models made full-coverage reading economical; structured-output techniques made 70-field extraction dependable; and orchestration frameworks made multi-step agent workflows an engineering discipline rather than a research exercise.

Source: measured run cost, June 2026; QA review cost estimated at 3–6 analyst-minutes per call06 / 18
SECTION 5SOLUTION CONCEPT
5 · SOLUTION CONCEPT AND DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Turn each conversation into structured economics; let nothing unverified reach a decision-maker.

The system converts each transcript into ~70 structured fields — what the call was about, how each phase consumed time, whether it was resolved, whether it was preventable — then aggregates those fields into indicators and priced reduction levers. Four principles shaped every component:

P1Analyse, don't act. The system reads conversations; it never touches customers or operator systems. This keeps the failure cost low (a re-run, not an incident) and lets autonomy be earned in stages.
P2Read everything, trust selectively. Full coverage is the point — but every extraction is quality-scored, and records that fail the score are excluded from indicators rather than averaged in.
P3Every machine decision on the record. Each agent logs what it decided, why, the evidence, and the alternatives it rejected — so the system can be audited the way a financial process is audited.
P4Match model size to task. A small fast model performs the high-volume reading; a large model reasons once over the aggregate. Cost scales with call volume; reasoning quality does not need to.
Sections 6–9 show how these principles are implemented; Sections 10–13 test them against a production run07 / 18
SECTION 6SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
6 · SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Six agents and an approval gate, arranged as a directed graph with controlled hand-offs.

The pipeline is implemented as a seven-node LangGraph state machine. Each node is a stateless agent: it receives the accumulated state, performs one responsibility, appends its outputs and decision records, and passes state forward immutably. A conditional route after quality scoring diverts failed datasets to a protective export path.

Why a graph rather than a script: conditional routing (the quality gate), retries, and checkpoint-resume require explicit state and edges. A failed batch resumes from its checkpoint rather than re-spending API budget — this mattered in practice during the validation run.

Why two models: extraction runs on Claude Haiku 4.5 (small, fast, ~$0.01/call at 50 calls/min); strategy synthesis runs once per batch on Llama 3.3 70B via NVIDIA NIM. Provider choice is a single configuration constant — pricing, rate limits, and key validation follow from it, so there is no vendor lock-in.

Stack: LangGraph · Claude Haiku 4.5 · Llama 3.3 70B (NVIDIA NIM) · Streamlit · FastAPI · 342-test CI suite08 / 18
SECTION 7THE AGENTS · 1–3
7 · THE AGENTS — ACQUISITION TO QUALITY

Each agent has one responsibility, explicit thresholds, and a reason to exist.

TABLE 7.1 — AGENTS 1–3: WHAT EACH DOES AND WHY IT WAS INCLUDED
AGENTWHAT IT DOESWHY IT WAS INCLUDED
1 · Data Ingestion Loads transcripts; rejects those under 150 characters or 4 turns; scans and redacts PII (emails, card numbers, identifiers) before any text reaches a model; logs each rejection and redaction. Bad input corrupts every downstream number, so quality gating happens first. PII redaction sits here — not later — so that no external model ever receives unmasked customer data.
2 · Extraction Submits each transcript to the extraction model with a ~70-field schema; scores its own output against 7 critical fields; below 70% coverage, issues up to 2 targeted re-queries for the missing fields only (the ReAct pattern), then merges. Models occasionally return incomplete JSON. Without self-correction those nulls silently bias indicators; re-querying only missing fields recovers them at ~1/8th the cost of a full re-read.
3 · Quality Audits every extraction on a 100-point model — completeness (30), enum validity (25), cross-field consistency (25), plausibility (20). Grades ≥85 HIGH, 60–84 MEDIUM, <60 LOW; LOW records are excluded from aggregation. A dataset-level gate routes catastrophic failures away from publication. An analysis system must know when its own output is untrustworthy. Consistency rules (e.g. "first-call-resolved" cannot coexist with "escalated") catch model errors that no single field check would find.
Thresholds shown are configuration constants, reviewable in one file; every decision these agents take is written to the decision log09 / 18
SECTION 8THE AGENTS · 4–6
8 · THE AGENTS — ECONOMICS TO PUBLICATION

From verified fields to indicators, strategy, and an audit trail.

TABLE 8.1 — AGENTS 4–6 AND THE APPROVAL GATE
AGENTWHAT IT DOESWHY IT WAS INCLUDED
4 · Aggregation Computes indicators (FCR, avoidability, resolvability, escalation, phase timings), distributions, and the three-lever economic model from QA-passed records only. Indicators built on unverified records inherit their errors. Restricting aggregation to graded records is what makes the numbers defensible in front of a finance audience.
5 · Insights A 70B model analyses the aggregate, then a second pass critiques each recommendation for data-grounding and specificity, then a third pass rewrites the weak ones. Falls back NIM → Claude → rule-based if providers fail. Single-pass LLM strategy output tends toward generic consulting language. The critique pass measurably removes it; the fallback chain means the pipeline completes even with two providers down.
Approval gate Optional human sign-off node between insights and export, with a timeout policy. Some organisations require a person in the loop before anything is published. The gate makes that a configuration choice rather than a process bolted on later.
6 · Export Writes dashboard data, per-call CSV, QA report, decision log, and an immutable run manifest. On the quality-failure path, preserves the last verified dashboard rather than overwriting it. The manifest makes every run reproducible and disputable. The protective failure path exists because a blank or corrupted executive dashboard destroys more trust than a delayed one.
Per-pass model usage is metered and reported; insights record their provider, pass count, and critique verdict in the output itself10 / 18

LIMITATIONS — STATED BEFORE THE RESULTS, NOT AFTER

L1. Corpus calls are shorter and simpler than live operator traffic (2:33 average vs a typical 4–8 minutes) and contain almost no hold time. Results demonstrate the measurement method; they do not predict any operator's baseline.

L2. Avoidability and resolvability rates in synthetic data run high. Treat the percentages as an existence proof of the classification, not as industry estimates.

L3. Economic figures in Section 13 combine measured rates with stated planning assumptions; both are labelled.

13 · ECONOMIC INTERPRETATION

SECTION 15CONCLUSIONS
15 · CONCLUSIONS

What the work demonstrates.

C1Full-coverage conversation analysis is economically viable today. Measured at $0.013 per call, reading every conversation costs roughly 0.2% of handling one — below the cost of the 2% human sample it replaces.
C2Structured extraction is dependable enough to steer by — when it is quality-gated. Zero failures across the run; every record carries a quality grade, and only graded records reach the indicators.
C3Phase-level timing reveals structure that duration metrics cannot. Roughly 40% of call time precedes problem-solving — a finding invisible to ACD data and directly addressable by known interventions.
C4A material share of contacts is classifiable as preventable or automatable, call by call. The classifications arrive as lists of named calls with intent and product context — implementable, not just reportable.
C5Agentic autonomy can be governed to audit standard. Budget ceilings, quality gates, PII boundaries, and a complete decision log held under production conditions, including a provider failure absorbed without intervention.
Each conclusion is traceable to the sections above and to the versioned artifacts of the June 2026 run17 / 18
SECTION 16NEXT STEP
16 · RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP

A four-week evidence phase on operator data.

The corpus results establish that the method works; they deliberately do not claim what any operator's numbers will be. The appropriate next step is the smallest decision that resolves that question: phase 0 of Section 14 — thirty to ninety days of historical transcripts, analysed under the governance described in Section 9, returning the operator's own call anatomy, indicators, and sized levers.

It requires a transcript export and a data-protection review. It requires no system integration, no customer-facing change, and no commitment beyond the four weeks.
